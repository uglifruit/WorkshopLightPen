LightPen · RGB sensor wand

Wand Calibration Targets

Five full-screen targets in the order the card captures them. The wand's three gelled LDRs each see all three primaries, and the only way to undo that mixing is to measure it — which is what the red, green and blue steps are for. White pins each cell's power law; black sets the zero.

Before you start

  1. Turn off night mode / blue-light filter — it would bake a blue deficit into the calibration.
  2. Turn off auto-brightness, and don't touch brightness again until the sequence is finished.
  3. Work in a dim room, with the wand tip flat against the screen.
  4. On the card: hold the switch Down while powering on, then release. It now takes five captures, one per tap of Down.

Level only, without colour

Show the card white for the first four taps and black for the fifth. Four identical bright readings tell it you offered no colours, so it calibrates level and curve alone — no target page needed. It can always tell the two apart: white is the sum of the primaries, so a real red, green and blue always read clearly darker than white on at least one sensor.

The sequence

Worth knowing

  • Each tap of the card's switch averages about a third of a second. Hold the wand still through it.
  • For the last step, a matte black card beats screen black, which still leaks backlight. The card expects the same black reference in both of its calibrations.
  • Calibrate against the screen you'll actually scan. A different panel, or the same one in night mode, is a different set of primaries.
  • Before saving, the card blinks two LEDs or five, to say which calibration it decided you gave it. After the reboot it reports how well your gels separate colour: one LED for barely, five for cleanly.
